About

Background

My research sits at the intersection of CRISPR genome engineering, stem cell biology, and regenerative medicine. I am drawn to questions about how cells sense damage, make fate decisions, and in some tissues, rebuild what was lost.

I graduated with First Class Honours in Biochemistry from the University of Manchester. During a year-long research placement in the US, I gained hands-on experience across genome engineering, gene delivery, and cardiac biology in two leading labs.

At the Gladstone Institutes, I worked in Jennifer Doudna's lab to programme SuCas12a2's collateral cleavage activity into a selective cancer-cell killing platform, engineering protein variants, optimising lipid nanoparticle delivery, and building computational image analysis pipelines. At UCSF, I used CRISPRi and AAV9 delivery in neonatal mouse hearts to investigate Taco1's role in cardiac development.

Starting September 2026, I will join the Rouhani Lab at the Francis Crick Institute for my PhD, investigating how FOXO1 S22W gain-of-function mutations influence liver regeneration and cellular reprogramming using human iPSC-derived models.

RNA-Triggered Chromatin Shredding to Eliminate TP53-Mutant Cancer Cells

Programmed SuCas12a2's collateral DNA-cleavage activity to selectively kill cancer cells carrying undruggable TP53 mutations.

  • Engineered SuCas12a2 variants via rational design to boost catalytic activity
  • Designed and optimised lipid nanoparticle (LNP) protocols for in vitro and in vivo mRNA delivery
  • Built a computational pipeline for automated image segmentation and statistical validation
  • Characterised Cas12a2 orthologs via dropout screens to define PFS requirements and PAM/PFS preferences

UCSF · Huang Lab

CRISPRi Dissection of Taco1 in Cardiac Development & Regeneration

Investigated the role of Taco1 in cardiomyocyte proliferation using cardiac-specific CRISPRi knockdown and AAV9 delivery in neonatal mouse hearts.

  • Designed and cloned CRISPRi guide RNAs under a cardiac-specific (cTnT) promoter
  • Packaged constructs into high-titer AAV9 (>5×10¹² vg/µL) and delivered to P1 mouse hearts
  • Quantified cardiomyocyte proliferation, ploidy, and nucleation via immunostaining
  • Applied RNA Scope to examine gene expression asymmetry in bi-nucleated cardiomyocytes
